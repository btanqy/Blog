Points of Interest

Maokong



We got up here by car, in two way traffic through a road that was only one car wide. Also, there were plenty of people hiking up to Maokong, also on the same road. Though path seems pleasant, the heat and amount of mosquitos especially in greener areas like Maokong is just not worth it. However, there is plenty of wildlife along the road, including plenty of butterflies, lizards, and birds.


Most of the area is populated by tea farms and teahouses, so there isn't much else to do unless it's hiking. I did go to the Taipei Tea Promotion Center, which displays old machinery that are used for each step of the tea making process. They also have a nice garden in the back, but I didn't get to see it since it was raining - it went from sunny and beautiful to pouring in the span of half an hour. I think the most memorable experience was getting to sit down and talk to the tea farmer (I think volunteering) at the center, where they sat us down and let us try their signature Tieguanyin and Baozhong tea. We talked for about half an hour just chugging tea, learning about their process and leaves, and random stuff for half an hour before buying some of their tea leaves.


These is some of the best tea I've ever had. We learned that it's difficult to balance tea to get just the right amount of fragrance, ferment, dryness, and shape without having to steep the tea for too long to extract those qualities without the tea getting bitter. I got their Tieguanying and Baozhong teas, each one of the last few boxes of their award winning teas for the Taipei for $70. Since I bought directly from farmers I got a much better price than if I were to buy some in Taipei, and much worse, from America. I looked online and just the Baozhong itself was $110 online.

Shengkeng

Shengkeng doesn't really have much other than the old street, and is famous for their tofu. In fact, the entire street has a premium stank, mostly from stalls selling mala stinky tofu. However, it's not too far of a drive from Maokong and I think is pretty worth it for food, arts and crafts, and a few of shops specailizing in wooden items (pretty common everywhere else too though). When we got to Shengkeng, it was pouring. The worst part about rain in Taiwan isn't how wet you get, but how you get wet. Because of the climate, the rain is pretty much exactly at body temperature. Meaning I didn't realize I had a pool of water stuck in my shoes until I took them off later.

Raohe Night Market

Raohe Night Market is simply the superior night market. Compared to Shilin, the variety of food and ambeince has more character. Although there are tourist traps such as the stands selling cut fruit and grilled beef, there are a lot of good and classic finds as well. Since Raohe Night Market is very cramped, it is difficult to move around. However, there are some stalls where they have seating located behind them, giving you a chance to eat without having to move around for people to pass through.


I don't feel like many people know or think of going to the Keelung river, but it is both refreshing and relaxing to climb up and over the gate from Raohe Night Market to Keelung river. It's well lit and has many active people, even at night walking, biking, and running.

Food

Taiwan is famous for its food, and my Taiwanese relatives also agree that the only thing to do in Taiwan is eat food. All prices reflect everything that was ordered in the picture, with only a couple exceptions. Anything that is rated 6/10 or more, I liked it. Anything that is 8/10 or over, I thought was definitely worth it.

Yaoyue Teahouse


Rating: 6/10 | Price: $15-$24 | Location: Yaoyue Teahouse @ Maokong, Taipei
The food is pretty standard, nothing to write home about for the egg dish with tieguanyin leaves, edamame, stir friend squid with pork, or tofu. The tieguanyin sausage had an odd stewed pork flavor mixed with tea, whilst still having the essence of a taiwanese sausage.

Tieguanyin Tea Set


Rating: 6/10 | Price: $11 + $3pp | Location: Yaoyue Teahouse @ Maokong, Taipei
 Although it was great to experience how to make tea (pouring water into pot, and pot into pouring cup, and pouring cup into normal cup), the traditional tieguanyin leaves we ordered didn't really taste fragrant, and had a somewhat bitter aquafina-type aftertaste that I didn't enjoy. Take the time to travel to the Taiwan Tea Promotion Center instead, where they'll talk to you extensively about tea (we were there for like at least half an hour), offer lots of samples, and be able to buy quality, award-winning tea you won't find anywhere else in Taipei.

Tofu Ice Cream


Rating: 8/10 | Price: $1.70 | Location: 陳記豆腐養生恬點 @ Shengkeng, Taipei
If you're in Shengkeng, its worth the experience, but it tastes exactly what you expect it to be.

Central Market Seafood

Rating: 6/10 | Price: $18-30 | Location: Central Market Stir-Fry @ Zhongshan, Taipei

We saw a lot of what I can only assume are office workers that came here to eat and drink for dinner. Part of our order included shrimp and vegetable stir fry, squid and pork stir fry, and pork blood cake. It's very local and the food is decent!

Pepper Buns


Rating: 9/10 | Price: $2.10 | Location: Fuzhou Pepper Buns @ Raohe Night Market, Taipei
This place is very busy, and really is the only stall in all of Raohe that has a line. We got lucky and got our food in about 5 minutes. This place does indeed live up to the hype. It is crispy, fresh, hot, steamy, and peppery with a hint of sweetness from the onions. This is absolutely a must in Raohe Night Market.

Guabao


Rating: 8/10 | Price: $2.60 | Location: 林媽媽刈包 @ Raohe Night Market, Taipei
The taste and texture of the bun is very similar to a steamed bun, and contains two fatty pieces of braised pork, cilantro, peanuts, mustard greens, and some chili oil.

Peanut Ice Cream Roll


Rating: 10/10 | Price: $2.20 | Location: Raohe Night Market, Taipei
The roll has the consistencey and taste like a thin crepe, which is then filled with lots of peanut bits, cilantro, and two scoops of ice cream. None of this should work together but it actually tastes incredible - it is sweet, salty, and super fresh. This is also a must try in Raohe Night Market.

Street Corn


Rating: 2/10 | Price: $2.70 | Location: Raohe Night Market, Taipei
The flavors are nice, and is similar to popcorn chicken seasoning. However, the corn is dryer than beef jerky, while the cob has the consistency of beef jerky. Yes, the cob itself has somehow cooked to the point that I bit through and ate it without noticing.

Egg wrapped in Fishcake


Rating: 3/10 | Price: 5 sticks for $3.30 | Location: Raohe Night Market, Taipei
This is a quarter of a quail egg wrapped with fishcake, deep fried. It's very oily since it's soaked in oil for quite a while.

Tofu Pudding with Boba, Kidney Beans, Grass Jelly, Ai-yu


Rating: 4/10 | Price: $2.70 | Location: 鄉淳布丁豆花 @ Raohe Night Market, Taipei
Nothing special. The ice above the tofu pudding was very coarse.

Thick Shaobing with Egg, Thin Shaobing with Youtiao, Soy Drink, Peanut Rice Drink, Turnip Pastry



Rating: 9/10 | Price: $3-5 | Location: Fuhang Soy Milk @ Zhongzheng, Taipei
The shaobings are both phenomenal, but I prefer the thin one so it tastes less bready. Shaobing is pretty common in Taipei, so these items are slightly higher quality than a lot of already available competition, but people love to line up for it. I was in line for an hour. I also got the soy milk (pretty standard) and peanut rice drink, which is sweet and decently starchy due to the rice. It's kind of like drinking peanut butter, in a good way. I've never had it before trying it at Fuhang, but their turnip pastry is amazing. Savory, shredded bits of turnip paired perfectly with the flaky package. I didn't get to try it this time, but Fuhang is also famous for their salty soy milk which contains shrimp.

Pork Sandwich and Black Tea


Rating: 5/10 | Price: $1.70 | Location: Mos Burger @ Everywhere
Pork sandwich was kind of disappointing, but filling. However, the black tea is amazing. I didn't expect black tea from a fast food place to be so good, and if it weren't for the plethora of drinks available anywhere I would most definitely come back for the tea.

Meat-stuffed Tofu and Dongfen Noodles


Rating: 9/10 | Price: $2.30 | Location: Fishball Bonzai @ Jioufen, New Taipei City
This is unique to Jioufen and can't be found anywhere else. The tofu is stuffed with spicy meat, and sealed with fishcake and simmered. It has a slightly sweet sauce and is decently spicy. The dongfen noodles are also very bouncy and are fragrant because of the onions, green onions, and red garlic.

Conveyer Belt Sushi






Rating: 7/10 | Price: $40 | Location: Sushiro @ Zhongshan, Taipei
Very solid, fresh sushi. However, they don't have an english menu available. The price doesn't reflect the images above - we ordered much more than this.

Beef Bourginoun, French Tea, Chocolate Latte, and Fruit Layer Cake UPDATE LOCATION



Rating: 5/10 | Price: $23 | Location: Mr. Chu @ Yonghe, New Taipei City
This is a cat cafe, so the higher prices are to be expected. The drinks and food didn't feel particularly special, but were still delicious. The cake had crepe-like layers, with kiwi, dragonfruit, orange, and banana. I would recommend trying somewhere else.

Taro, Sweet Potato, and Mung Bean Balls


Rating: 9/10 | Price: $2.00 | Location: A Gan Yi Taro Ball @ Jioufen, New Taipei City
These are significantly difference in nearly every single way compared to other stores. The balls are incredibly bouncy, but in a way that seems closer to something like a chewy jelly, compared to other balls that always that are starchy and gritty. Nesting below the balls is coarse ice, mung beans, and red beans.

Jin Xuan Tea and Tea Snacks



Rating: 8/10 | Price: $20 + $3.30pp for tea, ~$3.30 per snack | Location: A-Mei Teahouse @ Jioufen, New Taipei City
The Jin Xuan tea is light and refreshing, but consuming too much can cause an upset stomach which is why tea snacks must be eaten along with the tea. The tea also smells like butter and does have hints of butter in taste for the first two steeps. The tea snacks we had were dried squid, edamame, and mung bean cake. The mung bean cake is spectacular - it is very sweet, sandy (which cakes the roof of your mouth), and has a pretty negligible amount of red bean inside. The cake pairs super well with tea.

Chocolate Latte


Rating: 6/10 | Price: $5 | Location: Coffee Law @ Da'an, Taipei
Solid chocolate milk! Ambience is fantastic. They are known for their watermelon coffee, but I don't drink coffee unfortunately.

Steamed Meat Bun


Rating: 9/10 | Price: $0.50 | Location: Yilan Gold Medal Buns @ Yingge Old Street, New Taipei City
The most freshly steamed meat buns served ever. I had to wait 20 minutes before attempting to eat it. The buns are perfectly pillowly, lightly sweet, and has a peppery filling. The inside also holds a lot of soup/meat juice.

Lemon Ai-yu Drink


Rating: 5/10 | Price: <$1 | Location: Yingge Old Street, New Taipei City
Kind of like a lemonade, but with tasteless jelly inside.

"Sushi" and Garlic Noodles


Rating: 3/10 | Price: $10 | Location: 阿婆壽司 @ Yingge Old Street, New Taipei City
This isn't poorly rated because the food tasted bad, but rather, it was an odd combination and confusing eating experience. First off, the tofu bags are surprisingly sweet. I usually love eating tofu bags but these are insanely sweet. The roll wrapped in egg contains rice, cucumber, mayo, pork floss, and ham. This seriously shouldn't work, and it almost does. The mayo is just way too sweet. The seaweed roll contains rice, pork floss, and some kind of crunchy melon. This was my favorite here. The garlic noodles were insanely garlicky - I thought it was super spicy when I first tasted it, only to find out it was just pure garlic flavor. Apparently, it's made with red garlic which gives it its unique kick. Alongside the noodles came some mushroom and a century egg, both had no flavor.

Taro Duck


Rating: 7/10 | Price: $13 | Location: Tsui Feng Yuan @ Songshan, Taipei City
I found this place because it had a Michelin recommendation. When I arrived, it was only elderly eating here, at which point I knew this was going to be good. Additionally, while I was waiting for my takeout, the waiter was super nice and gave me a cup of tea. The dish contains a thin layer of duck, covered in taro paste, then deep fried. Usually, this dish takes more than a day to prepare, and the price reflects the work needed to put into it. It is crunchy, savory, and to be honest, a bit too oily, but is otherwise a fantastic concept and delicious. It comes with some cabbage and dipping sauce. The dipping sauce has no flavor.

Grilled Fried Chicken


Rating: 4-9/10| Price: $3 | Location: 維縈家鄉碳烤雞排 @ Various Locations
Taiwan is also famous for frying a chicken cutlet, smothering it in a sweet sauce, then grilling the whole thing for some char. There are quite a few stores, but I think the best one is the stand outside of Shilin Night Market, where there's always a line and the chicken is both fresh and crispy. The chicken does have cartilage and bones when it was fried, so there is some expertise needed eating around them. Also, the amount of chicken they give is pretty insane. I also did try another location, and is excessively sweet and slightly bland.

Smoked Chicken Bread and Garlic Danish


Rating: 4/10 | Price: $2.80 | Location: Sunmerry @ Da'an, Taipei

The almond berry croissant had the perfect bready-flaky consistency, and was filled with chocolate and jam inside, but somehow wasn't overly sweet. I loved every bite of it! There was also a nice seating area inside, but I didn't stick around.

Great cake! It's a lemony pound cake with crunchy icing on top. They only allow 90 minutes to sit down though.

Sugar Cane Juice

Sorry, don't remember the price. Sugar cane juice is always good! It is fragrant, and very natural tasting.

Rating: 8/10 | Price: $7 | Location: 永和豆漿大王 @ Da'an Taipei

It is supposed to be flaky and salty with the pork floss wrapped in pastry, but this was perhaps the dryest, densest, and flavorless thing I've ever eaten. However, the youtiao was freshly fried, fragrant, and juicy (because of the oil - very bad for health, but delicious). The soy milk was also fresh. However, the star was the danbing, an egg rolled into a wrapper. I can't tell if it's been pan fried or steamed, but the wrapper maintains a super bouncy texture despite being fairly thick. Paired with soy sauce and vinegar, this is a fantastic dish.

Chien-Yen


Rating: 7/10 | Price: $18.30 | Location: Chien-Yen @ Tamsui, New Taipei City
This is a buffet hot pot place, and the price is for a single adult lunch. Going in initially it felt grand (locals say its mid though). They have somwhere around 10 sauces, 8 drink machines (each with multiple options), a cooler with drinks (including beer), open fridges for meats, veg, mushroom, fishcake, balls, seafood, and noodles, a meat slicing bar with several different kinds to choose from, about 20 hot dishes (dim sum, stir fried, fried, steamed, grilled), a full dessert cabinet (cakes, etc), and a freezer for scooping ice cream and ice cream bars. This would have gotten a significantly higher rating if the meat wasn't sliced super thick, were super cheap cuts, and had cartilage. I would say that this could get an additional point not including meat.

Lu Rou Fan


Rating: 10/10 | Price: $1.60 | Location: Braised Pork Rice @ Zhongzheng, Taipei
This is what Taiwan's all about. It is pretty much 80% of just fat dumped on rice, but the way they braise it beforehand is legendary and isn't reproduced anywhere else. It is a simple dish, with massive flavor. It is slightly sweet and very savory. Although the amount of meat they give doesn't look like a lot, it is actually perfect given how fatty the dish is, which requires more rice per bite, but not in a bad way.

To be perfectly fair, both the drink and waffles aren't overly sweet, and they do have a lot of fun snacks to pick up on the side. However, this is not a light snack. My favorite onigiri are probably the selection at FamilyMart, but there is good selection at literally every other convenience store too. The seaweed is preserved in plastic wrapping that allows it to stay crunchy, and the rice is always very fluffy despite being kept in a fridge for god knows how long. I've gotten this maybe 5 times my entire trip.

Rating: 7/10 | Price: $6 | Location: Chun Shui Tang @ Taichung
This is the original shop that "created" boba milk tea. I rarely drink milk tea, but you can taste that the tea leaves they use are slightly better than from commercial milk teas. There are plenty of food options too, including this spicy lu wei. This was my first time trying blood pork cake - it's pretty similar to tteokboki, just in cube form.

Some of the most unique ice cream I've ever had, and had a consistency more similar to gelato than ice cream! My favorite were the guava leaves which was bitter and fragrant and the cactus which was sweet and sour. The addition (included in price) of the butterfly cookie, mango, longan honey, and waffle bowl made this a super fun eating experience. When we ordered, they had an unbelievable amount of flavors and toppings. This bowl was the discounted, student price. Otherwise, this would be closer to $14.

Yakiniku Barbeque










Rating: 9/10 | Price: $120 | Location: 茶六燒肉堂 @ Taichung
The only thing holding this back is the meat plate (not the short rib next to it) that looks like it has insane marbling and can only taste just as delicious, which in reality was stringy in some places and not as tender as I thought it'd be. Everything else though, phenomenal. My favorites were the chicken salad, the short rib, the duck, and the mackerel. I still dream about the mackerel. I don't think I'd ever be able to replicate this simply because I wouldn't know where to find fish of this caliper. However, the most unique item was the salt dip. It had a chalky texture, somewhere between sand and flour, and was mostly salt with hints of sweetness and citrus flavor. I had to ask for more because I kept dipping everything into it. The almond pudding was fragrant and refreshing too!

This danbing was a godsend for my stomach since getting up to the top early left me hangry. Super crispy, fragrant, and packed with egg, this could have easily gotten another two points only if the sauce they put on there didn't taste like straight up candy. If it weren't for the other items, this place would be a 10/10 for the turkey rice alone. The turkey is super aromatic and refreshing. It seems like the rice is soaked with turkey oil and a sweet soy sauce, which both sounds and tastes somewhat simplistic but is masterfully put together. Recreating this flavor feels like it would be incredibly difficult. The pork heart and tongue were pretty chewy and flavorless, but the fish stew was really good! A fried piece of fish is soaked into broth along with onions, tofu, and beancurd and is spicy, oily, and has a refreshing seafood flavor.

Oil Rice

Having a raw egg on rice is relatively rare in Taiwan. I don't think the egg drastically changed the dish, since it was heavily lathered in pork oil and soy sauce. There are no other toppings, which makes it feel a little lacking, but otherwise, has fantastic flavor.

Fried Scallion Pancake

Unagi Rice Set

Some of the best roasted chicken, since they roast it in traditional ovens with longan wood. It pairs well with a lot of sauces and powders that they have available for dipping. We also ordered some of what tasted like bamboo shoots which were fantastic and fried fish which were not that great.

Blueberry Yogurt Drink

A trendy spot, with pretty decent drinks! I liked how the yogurt was more similar to greek yogurt than yoplait, so it was refreshing to have something that contained fruit that wasn't immensely sweet.

Xiaolongbao, Marinated Cucumbers, Eggplant, Spareribs, Green Beans



Rating: 9/10 | Price: $50 | Location: Din Tai Fung @ Zhongshan, Taipei
I honestly do believe Din Tai Fung is better in Taiwan than it is in America. Although dishes are smaller, the prices are much lower than they are in America too. The xiaolongbao are pillowy and have lots of juice, and my favorite of the side dishes was the eggplant, followed by the spareribs. Not pictured here is also a fried rice, which was also pretty good.

Yakiniku BBQ Sets


Rating: 7/10 | Price: ~$33 | Location: 肉執事 台北三創門市 @ Zhongzheng, Taipei
Pretty solid for the price (small sets $6.50-$8.00). I liked their chicken set more than the beef, mostly because it could char more and was pretty tender. With a set is included infinite drinks (guava lemon tea was awesome), a sauce bar (citrus soy sauce was great), an amazing chicken bone broth soup, and ice cream. Honestly, a large part of why I like this place is because of the drinks, sauces, soups, and ice cream, but the bbq is also pretty decent too!

Squid Ink Fishcake and Popcorn Chicken


Rating: 7/10 | Price: $3.50 | Location: J&G Chicken @ Many Locations
The fishcake tastes pretty standard, but I do like the popcorn chicken from here better than Popcorn Chicken King in Ximending. Although it has less crunch, the marinade is good and the meat is juicy. It does have cartilage though.

Evaporated Milk + Roasted Oolong Tea, Bubble Milk Tea



Rating: 9/10 | Price: $7.30 | Location: Comebuy Tea @ Taoyuan Airport
I wish I knew about this place before the airport going back home. They grind and steam tea leaves through these coffee-extraction-type machines, which are pretty loud. The tea itself has a much more complex profile and an entire step up from Chun Shui Tang.
