Chasing the Stars

Points of Interest

  • Lake Manzanita
  • Lake Siskiyou
  • Lava Beds National Monument
  • Jedidiah Smith, Redwoods National Park

The best conditions to see the stars are when nights are relatively long, weather conditions are clear, and light pollution is low. That means travelling when it is winter or spring, going more inland, staying away from most cities and towns, and going only when there is a new moon. Although conditions weren't perfect, it was still an amazing opportunity to take pictures out at Lake Manzanita, Lake Siskiyou, Lava Beds National Monument, and Jedidiah Smith, all in northern California in September. Nevertheless, is is infinitely better than places that I've thought about trying such as Lick Observatory and Griffith Observatory.

I used my Nikon D3300 with myu lens mostly at 18mm, between 800-3200 iso (mostly 1600) with most exposures between 30 seconds and just over a minute. Some of these pictures came out more blurry than I would have liked because the autofocus couldn't adjust to the stars, meaning I had to manually adjust the focus.

Lava Beds National Monument

Night of the new moon

I honestly thought it would be darker here, but there was a small town that had quite a bit of light pollution spilling out over the plateau. I mainly took pictures looking away from the town. These are also from our campsite - I didn't go out of the way to get to someplace special since it was way too dark.



This is a photo facing the town I was referring to - notice how the stars are a lot less bright in this direction.



We also definitely saw a few shooting stars this night, but maybe 5 times more planes. In most of the photos with planes, you can clearly see a dotted pattern where the plane flashes their lights on and off, in addition to red and green hues. In this picture however, the streak across the sky is thin and narrow (roughly 4 times thinner than the plane streaks in other photos) and doesn't have any additional red/green color, which makes me think this could very likely be a shooting star.



Jeiddiah Smith, Redwoods National Park

1 day after the new moon

I was least excited for this location mainly because Jedidiah Smith is located in a dense, redwood forest next to the coastline. That means lots of obstruction, including leaves, fog, rain, clouds, etc. I was happy to get this shot but otherwise wasn't expecting much more than this, so I really only have one good picture to share.
